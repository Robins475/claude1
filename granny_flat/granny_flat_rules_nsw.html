Can I build a granny flat?

The state rules for a secondary dwelling (granny flat) under the NSW Housing SEPP 2021. They apply across all of Sydney.

The 60-second check
  • Zoned residential (R1, R2, R3 or R4)
  • Block is at least 450 m²
  • Block is at least 12 m wide at the building line
  • Granny flat 60 m² or less
  • Bushfire rating is not BAL-40 or Flame Zone
  • Not a flood control lot, heritage item or conservation area

All ticks? You can likely use the fast route: a private certifier approves it in about 2–3 weeks, with no neighbour notification.

Any cross? It's not a dead end. Lodge a development application (DA) with your council instead.

Approval

Two ways to get approved

Fast route: Complying Development Certificate (CDC)

A private certifier approves it if every state standard is met. It usually takes about 10–20 business days, and neighbours aren't notified.

Council route: Development Application (DA)

For anything that doesn't fit the fast route. Your council assesses it against its local plans (LEP and DCP). It usually takes 40–90+ days, and neighbours are notified.

Some councils allow bigger granny flats or smaller blocks through a DA.

Rules for every granny flat

  • One main house + one granny flat per block
  • The block can't be subdivided, so you can't sell the flat separately
  • The state rules require no extra car parking
  • The block needs lawful access to a public road
  • Not allowed on R5 Large Lot Residential land through the fast route

Rear setbacks increase for parts of the building above 3.8 m high.

  • Height: 8.5 m maximum above existing ground level
  • Front: behind the main house's building line
  • Corner blocks: at least 3 m from a secondary road boundary
  • Next to a park or reserve: at least 3 m from that boundary
  • Private open space: at least 24 m², which can be shared with the main house
  • Energy: BASIX certificate required
Single-storey tipMost granny flats are single storey and under 3.8 m high, so on a typical 450–900 m² block you need 0.9 m on the sides and 3 m at the rear.
Housing SEPP 2021 · Schedule 14 / 6
Before you buy or build

What rules out the fast route

  • Bushfire rating BAL-40 or Flame Zone. On any bushfire-prone land, the flat must also meet Planning for Bush Fire Protection
  • Flood control lot
  • Heritage item or heritage conservation area
  • Block under 450 m², unless the flat is built inside the existing house
  • Sewer or easement where you want to build. Needs Sydney Water approval

How to check any address

  1. NSW Planning Portal Spatial Viewer (free): zoning, lot size, bushfire, flood and heritage maps
  2. Section 10.7 planning certificate from the council. It's already in the contract when you buy
  3. Survey or site plan: width at the building line and the boundaries
  4. Sydney Water "Tap in": where the sewer runs
  5. Bushfire (BAL) assessment if the land is bushfire-prone
  6. Free site check from a granny flat builder or certifier

Sources & limits

Where these rules come from

Checked October 2026 against the NSW Government's planning pages and the consolidated text of State Environmental Planning Policy (Housing) 2021: s.54 and Schedule 1 (secondary dwellings), and s.57 (bushfire).

Good to know

Rules change. Your certifier or council gives the final answer for a specific block.

Councils add their own rules on the DA route. For example, Lake Macquarie's plan asks for 16 m² of private open space.
